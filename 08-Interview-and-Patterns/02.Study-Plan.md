# Study Plan: From Beginner to Interview-Ready

## 🧠 How to Use This

This is a concrete roadmap through the whole repo. It assumes ~1–2 focused hours a day. Adjust
the pace to your life — the **order** matters more than the speed. Each week ends with a gate:
if you can't pass the self-checks, repeat before moving on.

> 💡 The biggest mistake is **passive reading**. You learn DSA by *doing* — attempting the 🎯
> Practice problems before reading their solutions, and re-deriving code from memory. Treat every
> "self-check" question as a real test.

## 📅 The 8-Week Plan

### Week 1 — Foundations
- Read all of [Module 00](../00-Foundations/README.md): Big-O, Recursion, Bit Manipulation.
- **Goal:** state the complexity of any loop/recursion; write basic recursion confidently.
- Do every practice problem in those three files.

### Week 2 — Linear Structures
- Read [Module 01](../01-Linear-Structures/README.md): Arrays → Hash Tables.
- **Focus:** [Hash Tables](../01-Linear-Structures/06.Hash-Tables.md) and the two-pointer idea —
  they appear in a third of all problems.
- **Goal:** turn an O(n²) brute force into O(n) with hashing.

### Week 3 — Trees & Heaps (part 1)
- [Binary Trees & BST](../02-Trees-and-Heaps/01.Binary-Trees-and-BST.md),
  [Traversals](../02-Trees-and-Heaps/02.Tree-Traversals.md),
  [Heaps](../02-Trees-and-Heaps/04.Heaps-and-Priority-Queues.md).
- **Goal:** write recursive and iterative traversals; use a priority queue for top-K.
- Skim AVL; you rarely implement it, but understand *why* balancing matters.

### Week 4 — Trees & Heaps (part 2) + Sorting/Searching
- [Tries](../02-Trees-and-Heaps/05.Tries.md), [Union-Find](../02-Trees-and-Heaps/06.Union-Find-DSU.md).
- [Module 04 Sorting](../04-Sorting/README.md): really understand **merge** and **quick** sort and
  *why* they differ; skim the rest.
- [Module 05 Searching](../05-Searching/README.md): **master [binary search](../05-Searching/02.Binary-Search.md)**,
  including binary-search-on-answer. This is high-frequency and bug-prone — drill it.

### Week 5 — Graphs
- Read [Module 03](../03-Graphs/README.md) in order.
- **Goal:** confidently write [BFS/DFS](../03-Graphs/02.BFS-and-DFS.md); know when to reach for
  Dijkstra, topo sort, MST, or Union-Find.
- Grid problems (islands, shortest path in a matrix) are graph problems in disguise — practice them.

### Week 6 — Paradigms (part 1)
- [Two pointers/sliding window](../06-Algorithm-Paradigms/01.Two-Pointers-and-Sliding-Window.md),
  [prefix sums](../06-Algorithm-Paradigms/02.Prefix-Sums.md),
  [divide & conquer](../06-Algorithm-Paradigms/03.Divide-and-Conquer.md),
  [backtracking](../06-Algorithm-Paradigms/04.Backtracking.md).
- **Goal:** recognize sliding-window and backtracking problems on sight.

### Week 7 — Paradigms (part 2): Greedy & DP
- [Greedy](../06-Algorithm-Paradigms/05.Greedy.md) then [Dynamic Programming](../06-Algorithm-Paradigms/06.Dynamic-Programming.md).
- **DP is the boss level.** Work the full progression on each practice problem: brute force →
  memoize → tabulate → optimize space. Don't rush.
- **Goal:** apply the DP framework (state → recurrence → base → fill) to a new problem.

### Week 8 — Strings, Patterns & Mock Interviews
- [Pattern Matching](../07-String-Algorithms/01.Pattern-Matching.md).
- Internalize the [Problem-Solving Patterns](01.Problem-Solving-Patterns.md) catalog.
- Do **timed mock problems** mixing topics; use the [Interview Cheat Sheet](../INTERVIEW_CHEATSHEET.md)
  for last-mile review.

## 🎯 Daily Routine

1. **Recall first** (5 min): without looking, list yesterday's topic's key idea + complexity.
2. **Learn** (30–40 min): read the day's file actively; cover the C# and reproduce the pseudocode.
3. **Practice** (30–40 min): attempt 2–3 problems *before* reading solutions; struggle ~15 min, then peek.
4. **Self-check** (5 min): answer the file's self-check questions out loud.

## ✅ Readiness Signals

You're interview-ready when you can:
- Glance at a problem and name 2–3 candidate [patterns](01.Problem-Solving-Patterns.md).
- State time/space complexity before writing code.
- Write [binary search](../05-Searching/02.Binary-Search.md), [BFS/DFS](../03-Graphs/02.BFS-and-DFS.md),
  and a basic [DP](../06-Algorithm-Paradigms/06.Dynamic-Programming.md) from memory, bug-free.
- Explain your thinking out loud — interviewers grade reasoning, not just the final code.

## 🧭 Shorter Tracks

- **One weekend (crash course):** Module 00 + skim each module README's tables + do the practice
  in [Module 06](../06-Algorithm-Paradigms/README.md) + read the [Cheat Sheet](../INTERVIEW_CHEATSHEET.md).
- **Two weeks (focused):** Weeks 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 above (skip the advanced trees/sorts you can look up).
